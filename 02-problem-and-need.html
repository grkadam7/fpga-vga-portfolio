The need we identified

Load-cell measurement over a wide load range needs the conditioned signal to stay at a useful level for the ADC at every load. A fixed-gain amplifier cannot do this: small signals use only a small part of the ADC range and lose resolution, while large signals saturate the amplifier or the ADC and distort. What is required is amplification that changes with the measured signal, controlled digitally — stated as a need, before any circuit is named.

Intended application — load-cell signal conditioning for a measurement and control system, for example a DC-motor-driven conveyor belt, where the FPGA both acquires the weight reading and drives an actuator from it.

Why change is needed

Limitations of fixed-gain amplifiers.

LimitationEffect
No automatic adjustmentCannot adapt to changing input amplitudes → inaccurate measurement
Gain too highLarge signals saturate the amplifier or ADC → distortion
Gain too lowSmall signals use only a small portion of the ADC range → reduced resolution

Background study

SourceWhat we learned from it
Nexys A7 reference manual — Artix-7 XADCOn-chip 12-bit ADC, input range 0–1 V, suitable for sensor signal acquisition.
CD4052 datasheetDual 4-channel analog multiplexer; selection of multiple feedback resistors to obtain different gain levels, controlled by digital select lines.
INA126 datasheetHigh input impedance, high CMRR — suited to bridge sensors such as load cells. Gain is set by a single external resistor, RG.
NE5532 datasheetLow-noise dual op-amp — the gain stage device chosen after the candidate comparison below.
5 kg load-cell datasheet4-wire strain-gauge bridge; output is ratiometric to the applied load, so it needs a stable excitation supply.
Candidate op-amp comparison (WS3 · B.2)NE5532 chosen over LM741, LM358 and TL081: 10 MHz GBW, 9 V/µs slew rate, 5 nV/√Hz noise and 100 dB CMRR, at low cost and 8-pin availability. The others give either too little bandwidth (LM741, LM358) or more noise and offset (TL081).
