The concept we chose

A non-inverting op-amp gain stage whose feedback resistor is switched by a CD4052 analog multiplexer under 2-bit FPGA control. Four gains — ×2, ×3.2, ×5.7 and ×11 — are selected by the code S1 S0.

Chosen because every gain follows from one resistor ratio (Av = 1 + RF/Rin), so each setting can be calculated, simulated and measured rather than trusted from a vendor's internal gain table.

Options considered

Four candidates were compared; the rejected ones stay on record with the reason they were set aside.

OptionStrengthTrade-off
Fixed-gain amplifierOne stage, few parts, trivial to verifyNo adaptation — small loads under-use the XADC range while large ones saturate it
Op-amp with CD4052-switched feedback resistors (chosen)Gain set by a resistor ratio; four levels from one 2-bit code; fully predictable and testableDiscrete settings only; a transient at every gain change
Digital potentiometer in the feedback pathCompact, fine steps, serial controlWiper resistance and tolerance add gain error; limited signal voltage range; lower bandwidth
Dedicated PGA ICGain set internally, few external partsFixed gain sets and a factory gain table; cost and local availability; hides the design work the artefact must show

NE5532 chosen for the lowest noise and highest CMRR of the set at low cost in an 8-pin package. The comparison chart quotes 10 MHz and 9 V/µs; the TI datasheet in the project files quotes 12 MHz and 5 V/µs — to be reconciled before the final review. A 1 kΩ source makes input bias current negligible: 200 nA × 1 kΩ = 0.2 mV.

Scope

In scope

  • 0–5 kg load-cell signal conditioning.
  • Four discrete gain levels from a 2-bit FPGA code.
  • FPGA generation of S1 S0 and XADC acquisition.
  • Verification of gain accuracy, bandwidth and switching transient.

Out of scope

  • Calibration to absolute weight units.
  • Closed-loop actuator control law.
  • PCB fabrication.
  • Temperature-compensated zero tracking.

Option sketches and the WS3 comparison sheet are held with the project files; the op-amp comparison is transcribed above.
