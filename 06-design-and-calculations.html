What this page has to prove

Four gains turn out to be four thresholds. The FPGA compares the 12-bit XADC word against 819, 1434 and 1884 and latches a 2-bit code at the end of each conversion. Everything below follows from that: the gain equation that turns the code into a resistance, the thresholds that choose the code, and the op-amp that has to hold the result inside the driver window.

Numbers are the ones the hardware uses; where a value is still an assumption, the card says so.

Calculation 02 · The 12-bit to 2-bit decision FR2, FR6

How a 4096-level measurement becomes a four-way gain choice, and why it is thresholds rather than truncation.

MethodWhat it would do hereVerdict
Direct truncation (MSB)code >> 10 puts the edges at 1024, 2048 and 3072 — 250, 500 and 750 mV. That leaves ×2 only the last 10 mV of a 711 mV span.Rejected: the bands are fixed by bit positions, not by the gain table.
Threshold / windowingThree comparators at the three input levels where the gain should change.Chosen. Three constants, one register, and the bands follow the gain table.
Lookup tableA 4096-entry ROM, one 2-bit value per code.Rejected: the same three comparisons after synthesis, with more fabric and more to verify.
Implementationdo_out[15:4] (the 12-bit result of VAUX3 on JXADC A13/A14) is compared on eoc_out; the result is registered in mux_select[1:0] and driven on S1 (C17) and S0 (D18). V_IN is measured before the VGA, so the decision always sees the pre-amp output.
What the 2 bits areA band index that selects a gain — not a compressed copy of the measurement. Nothing about the 12-bit value is discarded; the analog signal still carries its full resolution.
Known limitThe current Verilog has no hysteresis and no dwell: at a band edge one LSB of noise can flip S1 S0 on consecutive conversions, and the CD4052 follows immediately. A two-conversion confirmation or a ±4 LSB deadband is the fix.

Calculation 03 · Gain-stage op-amp FR4

NE5532 on ±5 V rails — the parameters that set accuracy, bandwidth and headroom.

ParameterNE5532What it means in this stage
Input offset voltage0.5 mV typ, 4 mV max at 25 °CAmplified with the signal: 4 mV becomes 44 mV at ×11, so the zero-load output carries an offset. Absolute-weight calibration is out of scope.
Supply voltage range±5 to ±15 V recommendedThe board runs at the minimum, ±5 V. The 2.20 V peak output sits well inside the swing available there.
Gain-bandwidth product12 MHz typ12 MHz / 11 ≈ 1.1 MHz closed-loop at the highest gain — far above the DC-to-slowly-varying load signal.
Slew rate5 V/µs typA 2.2 V output step takes ≈0.44 µs, so a load step is followed long before the next XADC conversion.
Input bias / noise / CMRR200 nA · 5 nV/√Hz · 100 dB1 kΩ source makes bias error 0.2 mV; noise is negligible against one XADC LSB (244 µV).
Device classNot rail-to-railInput common-mode and output swing each stop about 2–3 V short of the rails. The stage never enters that region at ±5 V with a 2.20 V peak. Whether the specification should call for a rail-to-rail part is still open with the mentor.

The WS3 comparison chart quotes 10 MHz and 9 V/µs; the TI datasheet (SLOS075K) quotes 12 MHz and 5 V/µs, and the datasheet values are used here. Candidates compared on page 3.

Calculation 05 · Digital control bus FR2, FR6

Two bits, and the level-translation question they raise.

EquationControl bits = ceil(log2(gain steps)) = ceil(log24) = 2 → S1, S0
Values3.3 V CMOS from the Artix-7; CD4052 select inputs
FR servedFR2, FR6
Risk notedThe CD4052 logic-high threshold is 0.7 × VDD = 3.5 V at 5 V, while the FPGA drives 3.3 V. Either run the mux at VDD = 3.3 V or level-shift the two select lines. Tracked on page 5.
TimingA code change re-routes a resistor inside the feedback path, so the code must be stable before the sample is taken. The current Verilog updates S1 S0 at the same eoc_out edge that starts the next conversion, with no dwell.
